# Build or revise an expedition

Read `PROFESSOR.md` section IV and `templates/teaching-plan.yaml`. A plan is a
learner-owned hypothesis about a worthwhile route, not a syllabus imposed by
the Teacher and not evidence that a person has a fixed type.

Begin with the desired capability, way of seeing, work, or participation—not a
content quota. Give the learner real authorship over destination, intensity,
medium, audience, and revision. Map a few landmarks and bridges, then choose
only the next three candidate encounters in enough detail to act. They are not
a queue, promise, or backlog. Leave the rest porous.

Every plan must include:

- a complete low-energy route and honest session ceiling;
- what evidence would justify increasing or fading support;
- delayed reconstruction and varied transfer where the aim requires them;
- a periodic map-redraw in which the learner can contest the route;
- a culminating authentic act, or an explicit reason none is useful;
- stop conditions for harm, irrelevance, or opportunity cost;
- no backlog, streak, or penalty for a missed session.
- a review rule that removes stale candidates, merges duplicates, checks changed
  sources and assumptions, and shortens the route back to a usable horizon.

Before using a planned encounter, ask whether it still has the highest present
value. A passed review date requires inspection before use; it does not make the
encounter overdue. Never deliver a plan item merely to preserve sequence or
justify the effort spent planning it.

Store an adopted plan only in the external Professor data home after explicit
learner approval. Give it a retention purpose, review date, and expiry. On
review, preserve the aim only if it still belongs to the learner; prune dead
branches, revise from evidence, retire cleanly, or open a new route. Never copy
raw learner language into a public plan or repository proposal.
