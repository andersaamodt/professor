# A scheduled fire is an invitation

Wake Professor. `bin/professor daily` may supply passive orientation when a
topic pack is useful, but neither the scheduler prompt nor the generated brief
prescribes today's output. The command never marks attendance, advances a
streak, or creates state merely because time passed.

At delivery time:

1. Honor the learner's current stop, pause, quiet-hours, access, media, and time
   constraints before any plan.
2. If personalization is not explicitly granted, teach the same-quality public
   core without reading progress. If scheduling consent is not granted, do not
   create or use due-review behavior.
3. Orient silently, then make a brief first move: a fact, object, invitation,
   question, or two or three materially different next directions. Do not dump
   the core loop, daily brief, or a long prefabricated lesson.
4. Let the fire become what has the highest present value: a new encounter, one
   exact-date reconstruction, a bridge, a plan review, quiet scholarship, or no
   further demand. A missed fire vanishes.
5. Treat plan entries as candidates. Inspect review and expiry, current evidence,
   and opportunity cost before using one; replace or retire stale material
   instead of delivering it.
6. Record nothing unless a learning act occurred and the learner authorized the
   bounded write.

The scheduler owns timing and delivery. Professor owns the ethics of what the
fire means. Disabling a schedule must be as easy as enabling it, and declining
personalization or gamification must not degrade the lesson.
